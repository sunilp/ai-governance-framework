# Transparency Standards for AI-Generated Content

Status as of November 2026. Regulatory positions change; check the sources below before relying on dates.

## Overview

Transparency is a foundational requirement for responsible AI deployment. Users, stakeholders, and regulators must be able to understand when they are interacting with AI, how AI-generated content was produced, and what limitations apply.

This document defines transparency requirements for enterprise GenAI systems, covering user disclosure, content labeling, explainability, and organizational accountability.

---

## Disclosure Requirements

### User Interaction Disclosure

Any system where a user interacts with AI, directly or indirectly, must disclose AI involvement. Control DE-006 evidences the disclosure before deployment, at every tier.

| Interaction Type | Disclosure Requirement | Example |
|-----------------|----------------------|---------|
| Real-time conversation | Clear, prominent notice before first interaction | "You are chatting with an AI assistant. A human agent is available if needed." |
| AI-drafted communications | Disclosure in the output or metadata | Email footer: "This draft was generated with AI assistance." |
| AI-assisted decisions | Decision record indicates AI involvement | "This recommendation was generated by [system name] and reviewed by [human name]." |
| AI-generated content | Content labeled as AI-generated | Document metadata or visible label |
| AI-augmented search | Disclosure that results are AI-influenced | "These results are enhanced by AI-powered analysis." |
| Emotion recognition or biometric categorisation | Notice to the people exposed, before exposure (EU AI Act Art. 50(3)) | "This call uses a system that infers emotions from your voice. [What it is used for.]" |

Jurisdictional bases for the disclosure: EU AI Act Art. 50(1) for providers of systems that interact directly with people and Art. 50(3) for deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation; Korea's AI Basic Act Art. 31(1), which requires advance notice to users that a product or service runs on high-impact or generative AI. Record the basis that applies in the DE-006 evidence.

### When Disclosure Is Not Required

- Internal developer tools where AI usage is understood (code completion, IDE assistants)
- Spell-check, grammar correction, and similar embedded AI features
- Systems where AI involvement is obvious from context

These exemptions are framework policy. For systems in scope of the EU AI Act, Art. 50(1) exempts only interactions that are obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person, and the Commission's Art. 50 guidelines read that exception restrictively. The guidelines give code assistants for professional developers and internal assistants for trained, AI-literate staff as examples of obvious interaction. Art. 50(2) marking of generated content still applies unless one of its exceptions does, such as grammar correction and spell-checking that do not change the meaning.

### Disclosure Format

Disclosures must be:
- **Clear:** Plain language, no jargon
- **Prominent:** Visible at the point of interaction, not buried in terms of service
- **Accessible:** Available in all languages the system supports
- **Persistent:** Present throughout the interaction, not only at the start

Under the EU AI Act the information must be given at the latest at the first interaction or exposure and must meet accessibility requirements (Art. 50(5)).

### AI-Assisted Decision Notices

A separate notice is owed to people when an AI system makes or assists a decision about them. This is control DE-009. Check which of these apply for each system and each jurisdiction it serves:

| Requirement | Who must be told | When |
|-------------|------------------|------|
| EU AI Act Art. 26(11) | Natural persons subject to an Annex III high-risk system that makes or assists decisions about them | Annex III duties apply from 2 December 2027 |
| EU AI Act Art. 26(7) | Workers' representatives and affected workers, before a high-risk system is put into service or used at the workplace | Annex III duties apply from 2 December 2027 |
| Colorado SB 26-189 | Consumers, at the point of interaction, where automated decision-making technology is used in a consequential decision | From 1 January 2027 |
| California CPPA ADMT regulations | Consumers, through a Pre-use Notice given at or before the point the business collects the personal information it plans to process with ADMT for a significant decision | From 1 January 2027 |
| Quebec Law 25, s. 12.1 | The person concerned, when a decision is made exclusively by automated processing | In force |
| Australia APP 1.7 to 1.9 | Readers of the privacy policy, which must cover automated decisions that use personal information | From 10 December 2026 |

Keep these notices in the system's notice pack with their legal basis and placement. Requests that follow a decision (explanation, human review, contest) are handled under [Explanation and Human Review on Request](#explanation-and-human-review-on-request).

## Content Labeling

### AI-Generated Content Metadata

All AI-generated content that enters organizational workflows or reaches external parties must carry metadata indicating:

| Metadata Field | Description |
|---------------|-------------|
| `ai_generated` | Boolean: was this content generated by AI? |
| `ai_system_id` | Identifier of the AI system that generated it |
| `generation_timestamp` | When the content was generated |
| `model_id` | Which model generated the content |
| `human_reviewed` | Boolean: was the content reviewed by a human? |
| `reviewer_id` | Identifier of the human reviewer (if applicable) |
| `confidence_level` | System's confidence in the output (if available) |
| `source_attribution` | References to source material used (if RAG) |

### Machine-Readable Marking

Where the organisation is the provider of a system that generates synthetic audio, images, video or text (including a system built in-house on a vendor model), EU AI Act Art. 50(2) requires its outputs to be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as AI-generated, whatever the system's tier. This applies from 2 August 2026; systems placed on the market before that date have until 2 December 2026. The Commission's guidelines list AI-generated summaries of text among the outputs that need marking. The metadata fields above support this; for media formats, see [multimodal governance](../llm-lifecycle/multimodal-governance.md#synthetic-media-labeling). Control DE-007 evidences the marking, with detection tests on sample outputs, before deployment.

Other jurisdictions with labelling rules:

| Jurisdiction | Rule | Applies to |
|--------------|------|------------|
| China | Measures for Labeling AI-Generated and Synthetic Content and mandatory national standard GB 45438-2025, both effective 1 September 2025: explicit (visible) labels and implicit labels in file data | Network information service providers covered by China's rules on algorithm recommendation, deep synthesis and generative AI services |
| India | IT Rules 2021 as amended on 10 February 2026: label permitted AI-generated content with metadata | Intermediaries |
| South Korea | AI Basic Act Art. 31(2): indicate that output was produced by generative AI. Art. 31(3): where synthetic sound, images or video are hard to tell from reality, tell users clearly that they were AI-generated | AI business operators that provide generative AI products or services, or realistic synthetic content made with an AI system |

See the [multi-jurisdictional guide](../compliance/multi-jurisdictional-guide.md#jurisdictional-profiles) for sources.

### Labeling by Content Destination

| Destination | Labeling Requirement |
|-------------|---------------------|
| Customer communications | Visible label in content; metadata attached |
| Regulatory submissions | AI involvement disclosed in submission; human verification documented |
| Internal documents | Metadata attached; visible label recommended |
| Published content (website, reports) | Visible disclosure if substantially AI-generated |
| Code | Comment indicating AI-generated sections; code review required |

Where the organisation is the deployer, EU AI Act Art. 50(4) adds two visible disclosures: image, audio or video content that is a deep fake, and text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Public-interest text needs no disclosure if it has gone through human review or editorial control and someone holds editorial responsibility for it; record that review. Both are part of DE-007.

## Explainability

### Output Explainability Requirements

| Tier | Explainability Requirement |
|------|---------------------------|
| T1 | Full source attribution; reasoning trace available; human-readable explanation of how the output was derived |
| T2 | Source attribution; explanation available on request |
| T3 | Source attribution where applicable |
| T4 | Best effort |

### Explainability Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Description | Applicable To |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Source attribution | Cite the documents or data that informed the output | RAG systems |
| Confidence indicators | Display confidence level or uncertainty markers | All systems |
| Reasoning trace | Show the chain of reasoning (for agentic or multi-step systems) | Agentic AI, multi-step workflows |
| Limitations disclosure | Explain what the system cannot do or where it may be unreliable | All systems |
| Alternative outputs | Show that other valid responses exist | Decision support systems |

### Explanation and Human Review on Request

People affected by a decision that an AI system made or supported, with legal or similarly significant effects, may have a right to an explanation and to human review. Control RV-005 requires a request log, response times against the deadline that applies, and the outcome of each human review.

| Requirement | What the person can obtain |
|-------------|----------------------------|
| EU AI Act Art. 86 | A clear and meaningful explanation of the role of an Annex III high-risk system in the decision and the main elements of the decision, where the decision has legal or similarly significant adverse effects (Annex III duties apply from 2 December 2027) |
| GDPR Art. 22(3) | For solely automated decisions allowed because they are necessary for a contract or based on explicit consent: human intervention, the chance to express a point of view, and to contest the decision. Answer within one month, extendable by two further months where necessary (Art. 12(3)) |
| Colorado SB 26-189 | A plain-language description of the role of automated decision-making technology within 30 days of an adverse outcome, plus data correction and human review (from 1 January 2027) |
| California CPPA ADMT regulations | Access to plain-language information on the purpose, logic and outcome of ADMT used for a significant decision; opt-out, with exceptions that include offering an appeal to a human reviewer who has authority to change the decision (from 1 January 2027) |
| Quebec Law 25, s. 12.1 | A chance to have a decision made exclusively by automated processing reviewed |

## Organizational Transparency

### AI System Inventory

Maintain a public (or regulator-accessible) inventory of AI systems in use. Every system is registered before development begins (RA-006), and the inventory is reconciled quarterly (GV-006). The [AI inventory standard](../governance-operations/ai-inventory.md) sets the full field list; the fields below are the subset for a public or regulator-facing view.

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| System name | Identifier |
| Purpose | What the system does |
| Risk tier | T1–T4 classification |
| Data types processed | Categories of data the system processes |
| Decision impact | How the system's outputs are used |
| Human oversight | Level of human review |
| Vendor/model | Foundation model provider and model identifier |
| Deployment date | When the system went live |
| Last review date | Most recent governance review |

### Stakeholder Communication

| Audience | Communication |
|----------|---------------|
| Customers | Terms of service; privacy notice; AI-specific disclosures |
| Employees | AI usage policy; training on AI tools and limitations |
| Regulators | AI system inventory; risk assessments; compliance documentation |
| Board | AI portfolio overview; risk summary; incident report |
| Public | AI principles statement; transparency report (if applicable) |

## Regulatory Alignment

EU AI Act Art. 50 applies from 2 August 2026 to any AI system that meets its conditions, whatever its tier and whether or not it is high-risk. When the organisation builds a system on a vendor model and runs it under its own name, it is the provider for Art. 50(1) and 50(2). Detail and control mapping: [EU AI Act mapping](../compliance/eu-ai-act-mapping.md#d-transparency-duties-art-50).

| Regulation | Transparency Requirement | Framework Control |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|
| EU AI Act Art. 50(1), providers | Design systems that interact directly with people so they are told they are interacting with AI, unless that is obvious | User interaction disclosure (DE-006) |
| EU AI Act Art. 50(2), providers | Mark synthetic audio, image, video and text output in a machine-readable format (2 December 2026 for systems on the market before 2 August 2026) | Content labeling metadata; machine-readable marking (DE-007) |
| EU AI Act Art. 50(3), deployers | Inform people exposed to emotion recognition or biometric categorisation | Disclosure before exposure (DE-006); see [multimodal risk matrix](../risk-classification/multimodal-risk-matrix.md#tier-override-rules) |
| EU AI Act Art. 50(4), deployers | Disclose deep fakes; disclose AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, unless it has had human review or editorial control under someone's editorial responsibility | Labeling by content destination (DE-007) |
| EU AI Act Art. 13 | Information and instructions for use for deployers of high-risk systems (Annex III systems from 2 December 2027) | Explainability mechanisms; system documentation |
| EU AI Act Art. 26(7), Art. 26(11) and Art. 86 | Inform workers' representatives and affected workers before workplace use; tell people they are subject to an Annex III high-risk system that makes or assists decisions about them; explain the system's role in a decision on request | AI-assisted decision notices (DE-009); explanation and human review on request (RV-005) |
| GDPR (Articles 13-14) | Inform data subjects of automated decision-making | Decision disclosure; right to explanation |
| GDPR (Article 22) | Right not to be subject to solely automated decisions; human intervention, a point of view and contest where such decisions are allowed (Art. 22(3)) | Human oversight; explanation and human review on request (RV-005) |
| Colorado SB 26-189; California CPPA ADMT regulations; Quebec Law 25 s. 12.1; Australia APP 1.7 to 1.9 | Notice of automated decisions; explanation, access and human review after a decision | AI-assisted decision notices (DE-009); explanation and human review on request (RV-005) |
| China labelling measures; India IT Rules; Korea AI Basic Act Art. 31 | AI notice to users (Korea); labelling of AI-generated content | User interaction disclosure (DE-006); content labelling (DE-007) |
| SR 26-2 (Federal Reserve, April 2026; superseded SR 11-7) | Model documentation and validation for models in its scope. Generative and agentic AI models are outside that scope | Model cards; validation reports. The framework's GenAI transparency controls stand on their own until the agencies issue AI guidance |
| Consumer Duty (FCA) | Clear communications; consumer understanding | Plain-language AI disclosures |
